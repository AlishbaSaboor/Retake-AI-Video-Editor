## (1) Can real exports come out the wrong length?

**Yes, but only when the machine is short of memory.** With 2+ GB free, all 12 measured exports were within about 40 ms. With a program deliberately using up memory (0.3–0.7 GB left), 5 of 37 were wrong:

- **Too long, 3 exports (+0.11, +0.13, +0.23 s).** The export checks once per screen refresh whether a segment has ended. If the browser freezes for 150–270 ms right at that moment, the recording keeps running through the freeze. The extra time is either silence over a frozen frame, or up to ~0.15 s of whatever comes next in the original video. In a real edit, that can be material you cut out.
- **Too short, 2 exports (−0.42, −0.52 s).** The recorder ran for the full length, but the file is missing its last ~0.5 s of both picture and sound. **Likely cause, not proven:** under load the video encoder falls behind, and whatever it hasn't finished when recording stops gets dropped.
- This also explains the two export tests that failed once each under load (0.39 s long, 0.30 s short).

**No fix made, because none is both cheap and safe yet:**
- For the too-short case, waiting a moment before stopping the recorder is cheap. But the cause isn't proven, and at about 2 failures in 37 exports, showing it works would take dozens of exports under memory pressure each way.
- For the too-long case, nothing that runs on the browser's main thread can react on time during a freeze. Muting the sound at the exact end would stop cut material being heard, but wouldn't fix the length, and it risks clipping the last few tens of milliseconds of kept audio.

The notes list these options, plus a cheap warning to the user when an export comes out the wrong length.
